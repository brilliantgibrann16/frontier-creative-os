## Decision

### Q1 — First-class consumers

The first-class consumers of the Frontier Specification Language are
exactly the three classes named in RFC-0001 Q1, affirmed jointly and
symmetrically: **humans authoring, tools consuming, and AI systems
doing both.** Each element is grounded in an already-recorded
artifact; excluding any class would contradict the ratified corpus:

1. **Humans authoring.** The language is designed collaboratively per
   the Maintainer's standing ruling (RFC-0001 §Recommendation), and
   contributors propose changes through the single governed path
   (Constitution Article 6).
2. **Tools consuming.** Under the decided identity I-D, programs are
   structured definitions "consumed by tools" (RFC-0001 I-A scope,
   selected inside I-D by ADR-0006 Decision 1), and RFC-0001 A-1 holds
   under every alternative: the specification is authoritative and
   implementations conform to it (P-1, L-2).
3. **AI systems doing both, symmetrically.** The Constitution's vision
   "treats intelligent systems, in whatever form they take, as
   first-class participants in the platform" (Article 2). Symmetry is
   the already-ratified discipline, not a new grant: "The AI layer
   holds no privileged interface; human and AI contributions traverse
   identical gates" (Blueprint §12, INV-19); "No privileged AI channel
   exists into authoritative artifacts" (Baseline P-4); the CN-23
   bounds were reaffirmed by the RFC-0016 rejection (A-A, ADR-0004).

Bounding rules, all pre-existing and merely restated here:

- No consumer class acquires defining authority. The specification
  remains the sole authority (A-1, P-1, L-2); a runtime holds zero
  defining authority (Baseline L-3); no implementation is designated a
  reference implementation with defining authority (R-A, ADR-0004).
- Consumer symmetry is stage-invariant under the ratified staging
  discipline (ADR-0006 Decision 3): stage transitions alter language
  capability, not the consumer classes; changing the consumer classes
  themselves would require its own RFC → ADR decision (CN-14).

### Q2 — Success criteria at year 1, year 5, year 20

The success criterion at every horizon is the ratified Article 1
metric: "The project measures success by the quality and traceability
of its engineering, not by speed of delivery." The three horizons are
instantiated by the ratified long-term goals (Constitution Article 10),
with progress indexed to the recorded phase roadmap and capability
stages — never to calendar deadlines, because the corpus records no
schedule, Article 1 expressly rejects delivery speed as the metric,
and Article 10 reviews goals "at the close of each phase":

1. **Year 1.** Every significant decision remains traceable from
   proposal to implementation (Article 10, goal 2); no unknown has
   been resolved implicitly (CN-14); Stage 0 definition/validation
   work (ADR-0006 Decision 3) has proceeded only through recorded
   RFC → ADR gates.
2. **Year 5.** The phase roadmap (Article 10, goal 1: Language Design
   through Production) has advanced only through recorded decisions,
   with any capability-stage transitions travelling the RFC → ADR path
   (ADR-0006 Decision 3); a contributor community operates under the
   constitution (Article 10, goal 3).
3. **Year 20.** Article 10, goal 4 holds: the ability to understand
   and modify any part of the system ten years after it was written is
   preserved — the mission's "decade-scale maintainability"
   (Article 1) demonstrated across the long horizon.

Recorded interactions honored without addition:

- The eventual versioning scheme must encode capability stages
  (RFC-0001 U-10 interaction; ADR-0006 Decisions 3 and 5). Indexing
  the horizons to stages and phases is consistent with that rule;
  nothing here decides the versioning scheme (RFC-0009 window).
- This decision creates **no calendar commitment and no new metric**.
  Attaching delivery dates to phases or stages would contradict
  Article 1 and would be invention beyond the corpus.

## Consequences

- Both ADR-0006 Decision 5 re-deferrals are discharged at their
  recorded DP-15 review point. No open question of RFC-0001 remains:
  Q1 and Q2 are decided here; Q3 and Q4 were decided in ADR-0006.
- RFC-0001's historical text is preserved unchanged. Per the ADR-0004
  convention, the `docs/rfc/README.md` disposition index plus the
  deciding ADRs are the authoritative disposition record; the index
  row for RFC-0001 is updated in this same change.
- The S04 blocked content items "purpose, consumers, success criteria"
  (specs/S04-language-definition.md §Scope) now all have recorded
  decisions to draw from (identity: ADR-0006; consumers and success
  criteria: this ADR). The spec text itself unblocks only through P03
  specification work and its `TODO(blocked-by…)` marker updates
  (WBS P03.6), not through this ADR.
- The S04 charter's consumers and success-criteria content can now be
  written; charter status is updated administratively in this same
  change (program record, Blueprint §7).
- The ADR-0006 caveat on the RFC-0005 window ("subject to the Q1
  re-deferral's review point") is cleared; the RFC-0005 full
  compatibility-policy decision window is fully open. The decision
  itself remains future work; nothing here pre-decides it.
- **No implementation is authorized by this ADR.** CN-14 and the
  G-SPEC gates stand: no syntax, grammar, or semantics drafting is
  entailed, and Wave-1 engineering remains blocked until S04 work
  ratifies through its own recorded path (G-SPEC(S04)).
- PROGRAM.md contains no Q1/Q2 register text; no PROGRAM.md change is
  required by this decision.
