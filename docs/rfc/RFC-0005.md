# RFC-0005 — Compatibility and Stability Policy

**Status:** In Review · **Date:** 2026-08-02 · **Resolves:** U-6
**Subsystem:** S4/S15 · **Decision blocked by RFC-0001 (D-01)**

## Summary

Decide what compatibility FCOS promises, to whom, and for how long:
source stability, artifact stability, tooling-contract stability, and the
deprecation discipline that governs their evolution.

## Motivation

U-6; Doctrine §9: absent explicit promises, consumers depend on every
observable behavior (OB-6, DP-19). The policy must exist *before* the
first public release, or accidental guarantees will be created that no
future policy can retract cheaply.

## Assumptions

- A-1: Promises live only in ratified specifications and release
  governance (CN-18), never in implementation behavior.
- A-2: The policy binds all official implementations equally (P-2).

## Design Space

| # | Policy family | Trade-offs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P-A | Strong backward compatibility from 1.0 (Go-style) | Maximal consumer trust; heavy cost on early design mistakes; demands near-perfect pre-1.0 discipline |
| P-B | Edition/epoch mechanism (Rust-style): breaking change batched behind opt-in editions | Evolution without ecosystem fracture; significant spec and tooling machinery; couples to U-10 |
| P-C | Semver-style: breaking changes allowed at major versions | Familiar; majors fragment ecosystems at scale |
| P-D | Pre-1.0 explicit instability window + one of P-A/P-B/P-C at 1.0 | Honest now, defers the real choice with a recorded review point (DP-15) |

## Open Questions

1. What is "observable surface" for promise purposes — spec-defined
   behavior only, or also diagnostics text, tooling contracts, SDK
   surfaces? (Feeds WP16/WP24 work.)
2. Deprecation: minimum notice period, and where is it recorded?
3. Does the policy cover the conformance suite's own stability
   (test-breaking suite updates)?

## Consequences

Undecidable before RFC-0001 (the identity fixes who the consumers are).
P-D's window declaration, however, is decidable immediately and
eliminates the accidental-guarantee hazard during design.

## Recommendation (non-binding)

Adopt P-D's instability declaration now by lightweight ADR; defer the
1.0 policy family to post-RFC-0001 with a recorded review point.
